# Zild WebBook Visual Bible

## Purpose

This document is the consistency authority for every new illustration in the WebBook. The artwork must enrich the reader’s mental model without changing the author’s wording or inventing visual facts that contradict the continuity bible.

## Shared art direction

All images use the same visual language: **archival speculative naturalism**. The work combines cinematic natural-history illustration, restrained concept art, and recovered expedition photography. Surfaces are tactile: oxidized metal, ash, wet bark, translucent membranes, scratched glass, worn polymer, and mineral deposits. Light is directional and practical, usually from a moon, instrument, ember, bioluminescent organism, or pulse.

The fixed palette is bone paper, ink black, Ark blue-black, mineral pulse blue `#2D6C78`, muted teal, oxidized copper, and occasional ember ochre. Mineral Pulse Blue identifies living systems and communication. Ochre is reserved for memory, warning, or human warmth. No neon cyberpunk glow, glossy superhero styling, generic space opera, or unrelated fantasy ornament.

Use a 3:2 or 16:9 editorial composition for chapter plates and a 4:3 or square composition for appendix plates. Leave quiet negative space for captions. Never render explanatory text inside an image. Captions and labels belong in Markdown and the app UI.

| Isaac Porter | Aging senior scientist; lean, weathered face; silver-gray hair; slightly compromised posture; practical dark field layers; worn archive cylinder or thermos when relevant. | Do not make him young, heroic, militarized, or cleanly futuristic. |

## Object anchors

|---|---|
| The Ark | Generation-scale evacuation vessel; brutalist biological engineering; dark ribbed corridors, cryogenic bays, rootlike conduits, instrument panels, hidden geometry. |
| Mirrorglass | A navigation/trajectory system shown as layered reflective panes, dark starfield refractions, and thin diagnostic lines; not a literal magical mirror. |
| Archive cylinder | Small worn metal cylinder with a warm amber internal glow and a recurring glyph; tactile, pocket-sized, never oversized. |
| Duct-beat | Usually visualized as a vibration, concentric dust ripple, instrument tremor, or five-point pulse trace; never as a literal musical note. |

| The Loom | Layered woven root/mineral/metal architecture; part network, part memory structure; never a simple machine room. |
| Memory shard | Small translucent mineral or glass fragment containing faint internal light; carried or buried with care. |
| Scavenger craft | Compact, repaired, asymmetrical atmospheric craft; patched hull, practical landing gear, unfamiliar but believable technology. |

| Rift / Loom zone | Structural fracture where forest, mineral, and buried architecture meet; pulse patterns visible through matter; beautiful but dangerous. |

## Consistency rules

Every image prompt must repeat the shared art direction and the relevant anchors. Recurring characters retain face, hair, silhouette, and clothing logic across scenes. Recurring objects retain proportions and material. Locations carry the same light logic and palette. If a chapter does not establish a character’s appearance directly, the image caption must describe the plate as an interpretive reconstruction rather than unquestionable canon.

Visuals should be added because they clarify a place, object, system, or emotional turn. Avoid decorative images that merely repeat the paragraph’s mood. Each image gets a restrained caption and, where useful, a cross-reference to an appendix file.
